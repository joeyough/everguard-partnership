What this is

The work in front of you is bigger than day-to-day IT. You are running current operations, separating technology from the former parent organization, standing up new offices and centers, building the digital side, and supporting technology for your own security clients. All at once.

This document is about who does what, how decisions get made, and what happens in the first ninety days. The money is near the end, where it belongs.

How we actually work

Rhythm Weekly, monthly, quarterly
  • A 30-minute working call each week. Same day, same time, cancelled only by you.
  • A short written summary at the end of each month: what shipped, what is next, what is stuck and what it is waiting on.
  • A working session at the end of the initial period to decide what comes next.
Response times By severity, not by promise
  • Down. Cameras, dispatch, access control or the phones are out: same business day, and a call back within two hours.
  • Degraded. Something works but badly, or one person is blocked: next business day.
  • Everything else. Requests, changes, new work: acknowledged within one business day and scheduled.
  • Standard hours are Monday to Friday, with Saturday by agreement. Wednesday after 6:00 PM and Sundays are generally unavailable unless separately agreed.
One point of contact In both directions

You name one person on your side who can approve work, and we name Keenan on ours. Requests that arrive any other way get logged and brought back to that person rather than quietly done. This is the single biggest reason technology spend gets away from an owner.

Changing the scope In writing, before the work

If something significant comes up that was not expected at the start and would substantially change the time, resources or responsibility involved, we stop and review it together first. Any change to scope or price is agreed in writing before work begins. No additional professional-service charges are incurred without prior discussion and approval.

Spending your money $500 approval line
  • Hardware, licenses, subscriptions and third-party services are yours, bought in your name, on your accounts.
  • Anything over $500, or any new recurring subscription of any size, needs your approval first.
  • We do not mark up equipment or vendor invoices. If a vendor pays a referral or partner fee on something we recommend, you are told before you buy it.

The first ninety days

October 1 to December 31, 2026. The period exists to prove the value and find the real rhythm, not to run out a clock.

DAYS 1 – 30

Get the map

  • Full inventory: systems, accounts, cameras, vendors, contracts, renewal dates.
  • Every account and credential moved into your name and a password manager you own.
  • The separation plan from the former parent organization, in writing.
  • First read on where money is leaking and what is fragile.
DAYS 31 – 60

Stop the bleeding

  • Migration work underway, in the order that removes risk first.
  • Duplicate and unused subscriptions cancelled, with the savings named.
  • Business-unit structure drafted: what belongs under which entity.
  • The two or three processes that eat your week, documented and handed off.
DAYS 61 – 90

Build the next thing

  • Office, surveillance and dispatch technology moving on a dated plan.
  • Web and client-facing systems live or scheduled.
  • A written roadmap for the next two quarters, with owners and dates.
  • The review: workload, value delivered, and the service level that fits.
How we will know it worked

At the review we will look at four things: the separation is done or dated, your recurring technology spend is lower than it is today or explained, the number of things only you can answer has gone down, and the next two quarters have a plan with names on it.

What stays yours

This is the part most technology agreements leave vague, and it is the part that costs an owner the most when a relationship ends.

  • Accounts and credentials. Every account is created in your name and under your domain. You hold the ownership seat on every platform. Nothing is registered under a vendor's account.
  • Your data. Footage, logs, client records, documents and anything produced for your business are yours, during and after.
  • Work product. Configurations, documentation, runbooks, site plans and websites built for you are yours to keep and to hand to anyone else.
  • Our tools stay ours. General methods, templates and internal tooling we bring with us remain ours, and you keep a license to whatever is installed for you.
  • Leaving well. If either side ends this, we give you up to 30 days of transition help at no additional monthly fee: documentation, credential handover, and a call with whoever comes next.

The boundaries

Included Professional labor

For Ownership and Transformation, professional labor on the agreed initiatives is included in the monthly fee. The point is a partnership, not a stream of surprise invoices.

Not included External costs

Unless stated otherwise, monthly fees exclude hardware, equipment, software licenses and subscriptions, hosting, domains, construction, cabling, third-party contractors and vendor charges, materials, significant travel, and other external project costs.

Help from others Subcontractors

We may bring in specialists for specific work. You are told who and why. Keenan remains responsible for the result and for anyone he brings in, and anyone who touches your systems is under the same confidentiality terms we are.

Confidentiality Both directions

What we learn about your business, your clients and your sites stays with us, during and after. The same applies to what you learn about how we work. Neither side uses the other's client list. Neither side hires the other's people during the engagement or for twelve months after, unless both agree in writing.

Insurance and independence Separate businesses

Keenan Miller Technology Solutions and Joe CXO are independent businesses, not employees, and each carries its own insurance. Neither firm holds an exclusive claim on your security clients or your other vendors.
